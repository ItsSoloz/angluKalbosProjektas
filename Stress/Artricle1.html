Is school stress just part of growing up, or a crisis we ignore?

Herkus Korsakas 2026-11-12

Introduction

High school can feel like a never-ending marathon where grades decide your worth. As 11th graders, we face an endless wave of tests, projects, and growing expectations about our future. I have chosen this topic because stress and mental health struggles are a daily reality for teenagers, yet we rarely talk about them openly.

The Weight of Exam Stress

Exams can be mentally exhausting for students. Constant pressure from teachers, parents, and our own high expectations builds up a lot of stress. You might lose sleep, overthink every mistake, or feel completely burned out before the week ends. This chronic stress drains our physical energy, mood, and overall well-being.

How to Take Care of Yourself

Managing this pressure requires discipline and self-care habits. We should always prioritize our own mental and physical health. Simple actions like taking short breaks while studying, getting enough sleep, going for a walk, or sharing our worries with friends makes a huge difference. Students need to understand that they are much more than just a grade.

When to Seek Help

Sometimes self-care is not enough, and that is okay. If stress turns into persistent sadness, panic attacks, or hopelessness, we should reach out for support. Talking to a friend, a trusted teacher, or parents can help us a lot with stress management. Asking for help is not a sign of weakness, it is a sign of strength.

Conclusion

To conclude, exam and overall school stress is a normal part of growing up, but it should never control our lives. By protecting our mental health, we can get through these challenging years.
