What Makes a True Friend in Your Teenage Years?

Nojus Strimaitis 2026-11-12

Introduction

I have chosen the topic of friendship because I think it is a big part of everyone's teenage years. At this age, we meet new people, make connections and learn more about ourselves. However, having many people around us does not always mean we have real friends. Sometimes it is hard to understand who truly cares about us and who is only there when things are going well.

Real vs. Superficial Friendships

In my opinion, a real friend is someone you can be yourself around without feeling judged. They listen and support you when you are having a bad day and are happy for you when something good happens. Furthermore, you do not have to talk every single day to stay close. Real friendship is about trust, honesty and being there for each other. Although superficial friendships can be fun, they are often based on popularity, appearance or common interests. As a result, they may not last when problems appear. I believe the best way to make real friends is to be yourself, be kind, show interest in others and not try too hard to impress anyone.

How Friendships Change Over Time

I'd also like to add that friendships change as we grow up. We meet new people, develop different interests and sometimes drift apart from friends we used to be very close with. Although this can be sad, it is a normal part of life. Some friendships become stronger with time, while others slowly fade away.

Conclusion

All in all, I believe this topic is important and relevant to young people because friendship can have a big influence on our lives. Understanding what makes a true friend can help us build stronger relationships and realise that having a few real friends is much more valuable than having lots of people who do not really know us.
